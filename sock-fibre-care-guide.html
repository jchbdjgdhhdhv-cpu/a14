Guide 02

Sock Fibres & Care

The fibre decides how a ribbed sock feels and how long its spring lasts. Care decides the rest.

In this guide

  1. 01Why blends work
  2. 02Choosing the main fibre
  3. 03Washing ribbed socks
  4. 04Drying without stretching
  5. 05Storing and rotating
01

Why blends work

A pure fibre sock rarely holds its shape. Most good socks blend a main fibre for comfort with a little nylon for strength and a touch of elastane so the rib keeps recovering.

02

Choosing the main fibre

  • Merino wool: warm in cold, comfortable in warmth, resists odour.
  • Cotton: soft and breathable, best for mild days.
  • Bamboo viscose: silky and cool, with a soft drape.
  • Alpaca: very warm and light, for the coldest weather.
03

Washing ribbed socks

Turn socks inside out and wash cool, at 30°C or below, with a gentle detergent. Skip fabric softener, which coats fibres and dulls the rib’s spring. Wash wool with a wool-safe detergent.

04

Drying without stretching

Heat is the enemy of elastane. Dry flat or hang by the toe, never on a hot radiator, and avoid the tumble dryer where you can. A ribbed cuff dried hot loses its grip quickly.

05

Storing and rotating

Fold socks in pairs rather than balling them, which keeps the cuff stretched. Rotating several pairs gives fibres time to recover between wears, and every pair lasts longer.
