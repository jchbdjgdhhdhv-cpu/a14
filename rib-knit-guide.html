Guide 01

Rib Knit Explained

Every ribbed sock is built from two stitches. How they are arranged decides how the sock stretches, grips and looks.

In this guide

  2. 02Reading the ratio
  3. 03Where the rib goes

  5. 05Fit and stretch
01

Knit and purl, side by side

A knit stitch sits forward and a purl stitch sits back. Arrange them in alternating columns and the purls recede, pulling the knits together. Relaxed, the fabric looks narrow; stretched, it opens up.

02

Reading the ratio

  • 1×1: one knit, one purl. Fine, stretchy and neat, common on cuffs.
  • 2×2: bolder columns, a classic sporty look with good grip.
  • 3×1 and 4×2: wider knit columns, a dressier look with a little less give.
03

Where the rib goes

Most socks rib the cuff so it stays up without digging in. Some rib the whole leg for a fitted look, and many add a ribbed arch band to hold the sock in place inside the shoe.

04

Signs of a good rib

  • The cuff springs back instantly when released.
  • Columns run straight, not twisted around the leg.
  • The cuff leaves no deep mark after a day’s wear.
  • The heel and toe are reinforced and smoothly linked.
05

Fit and stretch

Rib is forgiving but not limitless. If a cuff digs in, size up; if it slides down, the rib may be stretched out or the elastane worn. Fully ribbed socks suit narrow and wide calves alike.
